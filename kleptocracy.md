Kleptocracy Since Ronald Reagan

The term kleptocracy—governance by those who use public office for personal wealth and private gain—
is frequently used by political scientists, legal scholars, and corruption watchdogs to describe the 
structural shifts under the Trump administration, particularly during his second presidential term. 
While traditional American corruption historically involved hidden or backroom deals, scholars 
note that the Trump administration has shifted toward an overt, institutionalized model where 
conflicts of interest are openly normalized.

The primary policy shifts, actions, and institutional 
changes that critics point to as evidence of a burgeoning American kleptocracy include the 
following:

1. De-prioritizing Anti-Corruption Enforcement
  
  Disbanding anti-kleptocracy units: 
  The administration disbanded the Department of Justice’s (DOJ) National Cryptocurrency 
  Enforcement Team and ordered its Market Integrity and Major Frauds Unit to stop investigating 
  the sector.
  
  Pausing the FCPA: 
  The administration ordered a halt on new investigations and enforcement of the Foreign Corrupt 
  Practices Act (FCPA), removing a critical global deterrent against corporate bribery and foreign graft.
  
  Sidelining Public Integrity: The DOJ's Public Integrity Section, tasked with prosecuting political 
  corruption, was heavily downsized, stripping its ability to independently file new cases against 
  public officials.
  
2. Commercialization of Public Office & "Web3 Grifting"

  Crypto and Memecoins: Family businesses linked to the administration actively mint Trump-themed 
  meme coins and corporate stablecoins while concurrently rolling back federal crypto regulations 
  and settling ongoing SEC enforcement actions.
  
  Foreign State Gifts: High-profile, multi-million-dollar transactions from foreign monarchies and 
  governments—such as state-gifted aircraft—have been legally approved by administration officials.
  
  Corporate Slush Funds: Critics highlight the monetization of access, where donors and holders of 
  personal digital assets are granted direct access to private political functions and executive 
  policy-makers.
  
3. Civil Service Purges and Institutional Dismantling

  Dismantling Aid Agencies: The administration effectively dismantled the U.S. Agency for International 
  Development (USAID), which historically managed overseas foreign assistance.
  
  Diplomatic and NSC Downsizing: Over 1,350 career foreign service officers and employees were dismissed, 
  and the National Security Council (NSC) staff was aggressively downsized to a fraction of its former 
  scale.
  
  Replacing Experts with Loyalists: Aggressive overhauls to the civil service framework allow the 
  administration to purge independent, non-partisan experts and fill regulatory agencies with 
  political operatives.
  
4. Weaponization of Presidential Pardons

  Pardoning Financial Criminals: The administration has frequently used its executive pardon power to 
  grant clemency to individuals convicted of major international financial crimes and large-scale 
  cyber fraud.
  
  Shielding Political Allies: Executive immunity and blanket pardons have been systematically extended 
  to political cronies, creating an environment that legal watchdogs describe as an established culture 
  of absolute impunity.
  
5. Systematic Real Estate and Corporate Self-Dealing

  Monetizing Official Properties: The use of Trump-owned hotels, clubs, and resorts for official government 
  business and foreign diplomatic events funnels millions in taxpayer and foreign state funds directly into 
  private family assets.

  Unscrutinized Public Vehicles: The utilization of Special Purpose Acquisition Companies (SPACs) 
  to take family media entities public allows the administration to bypass traditional SEC disclosure 
  regulations, creating direct conduits for speculative asset-buying by special interest groups.
